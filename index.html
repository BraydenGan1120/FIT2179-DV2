Australia in the AI Race

Artificial intelligence is reshaping the global economy, pushing countries to compete for innovation, talent, infrastructure, and technological leadership. This visual story explores where Australia stands globally and how AI adoption differs across states, territories, and industries.

Section One

The Global Race: Where Does Australia Stand?

AI is no longer a future technology. It is already changing how governments deliver services, how businesses compete, and how workers make decisions. The Government AI Readiness Index compares how prepared countries are to adopt and govern AI at scale.

Australia ranks strongly among advanced economies, supported by universities, digital infrastructure, and policy momentum. However, larger AI markets such as the United States and China continue to set the pace through deeper investment and larger technology ecosystems.

Section Two

Inside Australia

Inside Australia, the AI story is not evenly distributed. Adoption rates vary significantly across states and territories due to differences in industry focus, business size, digital infrastructure, workforce capability, and local innovation ecosystems.


States with stronger technology sectors, larger urban populations, and greater investment in digital transformation tend to adopt AI more rapidly, while regional and less densely populated areas often face slower uptake because of limited resources, skills shortages, and reduced access to advanced technologies.

Section Three

What's Holding Australia Back?

Despite its stable global ranking, Australia's actual AI adoption rate lags well behind its readiness score. Businesses cite cost, skills shortages, and distrust as key blockers. And when it comes to public confidence in AI, Australians are among the most sceptical in the developed world.

This section explores more about why.

Barriers to Adoption

Australia's AI readiness ranking suggests strong digital capability and infrastructure, yet public trust in AI remains far more cautious. While businesses and governments continue investing in AI technologies, many Australians remain concerned about reliability, safeguards, and the risks associated with rapid AI adoption.


This chart highlights how trust-related concerns consistently score higher than optimism indicators, reinforcing the idea that scepticism remains a major barrier to wider AI adoption across the country.

Source: University of Melbourne/KPMG Trust in AI report

Capability and Confidence in AI Adoption

Australia has seen major growth in AI adoption between 2022 and 2024, especially in organisations and employee use at work. However, areas like trust in Awareness, Trust, AI knowledge, and support from organisations have changed very little or decreased. This shows that while AI is becoming more common in workplaces, confidence and understanding of the technology are not increasing at the same pace.

Sector Readiness

Australia’s AI readiness is uneven across industries, with stronger performance in IT, infrastructure, and client-focused applications, while workforce skills and marketing capabilities remain weaker in several sectors. Finance, Education and mining show the highest overall readiness, whereas retail and manufacturing still face capability gaps that may slow large-scale AI adoption.
